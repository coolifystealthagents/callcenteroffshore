# What share of repeat callers receive a routed owner before the third contact?

## Question and scope

This protocol asks what the repeat caller routing record can show about evidence, timing, and closure at Call Center Offshore. It is a local measurement method, not an industry benchmark or an audit opinion. The result describes one defined population during one observation window. The method is for a support supervisor who needs a reproducible review of a delegated repeat caller routing workflow.

The scope of this repeat caller routing protocol is the population named below, not every call or customer in the center. Write the start event, the stop event, and the pause rule before collecting data. A count without a denominator is not a measurement, especially when the queue changes size during the day. Call Center Offshore records the window and every exclusion so a second reviewer can repeat the sample.

## Population and unit of analysis

The unit is one customer issue and its contact history. Test the definition on a case that crosses owners, a case that is still open, and a case that was reopened. Apply the same rule to every case and keep the rule beside the extract. A definition that changes halfway through the window destroys comparability.

The population is customers with two or more contacts about the same issue during the window. The primary measure is the share of cases that meet the written definition. Add a count of missing evidence and a median age for open cases. Report each measure with its denominator, observation window, and exclusion count.

## Definitions and measures

Define completion before the first case. For this protocol, completion means the record supports the measure with a dated source and a named reviewer. A case that fails the definition stays open even when a status field says closed. Call Center Offshore records the status field and the evidence separately so the difference remains visible.

Each case needs a source, an observation time, the field it supports, and the reviewer who checked it. Mark missing, unavailable, and confirmed negative values separately. Collapsing them hides the difference between no evidence and evidence of absence. The primary measure for repeat caller routing is the share of repeat contacts with a named owner and a documented next action, with the numerator and denominator attached.

## Evidence standard

Use individual accounts, suitable authentication, least-necessary permissions, and logged changes. Do not create shadow spreadsheets that become an ungoverned system of record. Store controlled links instead of copying sensitive content when a source can be referenced. Call Center Offshore names the support supervisor who may change policy, approve an exception, execute an action, and verify the result.

Every unresolved case needs one accountable person and a due condition. A group channel is not an owner, and apparent urgency does not expand authority. Separate who may change the instruction from who may approve a single exception. That separation protects the agent from being assigned consequential judgment through vague instructions.

## Sampling and collection

Use a random or systematic start rule, then take consecutive cases within each stratum. Stratify by risk, owner, and channel so the sample is not dominated by the easiest queue. Include at least one adverse case: a missing source, a conflict, an unavailable owner, or a deadline that forced a decision. Call Center Offshore records every access failure instead of replacing it silently.

Sample across the full observation window, including busy days and days when coverage changed. A sample drawn only from quiet days can make a fragile control look stable. Record the date and time of each observation. If the population is small, report the count and the instability instead of a precise-looking rate.

## Limits and reporting

Show the distribution, not only the mean. A median close time can hide a long tail of cases that nobody reviews. Report the open-case count and the oldest case age by owner. That view shows where delay accumulates and which queue needs a process change.

The main limit is that the repeat caller routing record may be incomplete, delayed, or maintained outside the team. This method cannot infer missing work from a clean summary. It also cannot establish whether a policy was followed in cases that left no trace. Call Center Offshore states those limits before presenting the result and labels any change in the definition.
