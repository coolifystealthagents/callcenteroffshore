# Call center knowledge article expiry: deciding when guidance goes stale
*August 20, 2026*

A knowledge article can become unsafe before anyone notices that its review date passed. A product changes, a queue gains a new authority boundary, a system field is renamed, or a customer-facing promise is revised. A call center should treat article expiry as a work decision: whether representatives may continue using the guidance, whether a temporary note is needed, and which owner verifies the replacement. A date in a library is not a control unless it changes what the queue does.

## Define staleness by risk

Not every article needs the same review interval. Guidance that affects identity checks, refunds, sensitive changes, legal language, or safety should have a stronger trigger and a clear stop rule. A low-risk explanation may be reviewed after a longer interval, but a known system change should expire it immediately. Record why an interval was selected rather than applying one calendar rule to every page.

Use expiry triggers beyond time. A customer correction, repeated escalation, script change, product release, system-field change, or quality finding can send an article to review. Let representatives flag an article when the workflow they see does not match the instruction. Make the flag easy to route and assign an owner. If people must write a long explanation before reporting a conflict, stale guidance will remain in use.

## Make status visible at work time

A representative should see whether an article is current, under review, replaced, or temporarily restricted. If an article expires while the queue is open, define what the agent may do. The safe choice may be to use a short approved interim instruction, transfer the call, or preserve the question for a manager. Do not tell agents simply to use judgment where the organization has not defined the boundary.

Version control needs an effective point. Publish a change note that says what changed, which call reasons are affected, and what examples are no longer valid. Retire or clearly mark the old version so a printed copy or saved link does not compete with the current answer. Ask supervisors to check a sample after the change and record whether the new guidance was available and understandable.

## Assign ownership close to the process

The article writer may not own the policy. Name the subject owner, queue owner, and reviewer who can approve a replacement. For an article that crosses several queues, record who resolves disagreement. The representative needs one route for an uncertain answer, not a debate among hidden editors. Keep the source of the approved instruction visible to the reviewer so the article does not gradually become an unsupported summary.

Use customer contacts as evidence. Repeated questions, corrections, transfers, and complaints may indicate that an article is stale or that the queue is using it differently. Read the original calls before editing the text. A shorter article is not automatically better if it removes the stop condition or changes a manager decision into an agent action.

## Measure expiry discipline

Track overdue reviews, articles used after expiry, flags awaiting response, repeat conflicts, and quality findings linked to guidance. Review a small set of current articles and ask a representative to explain the next action in an ambiguous case. If the answer varies, update the example or the route. Do not treat a completed review checkbox as proof that the guidance works.

A call center knowledge-article expiry routine is useful when staleness changes behavior, every uncertain answer has an owner, old versions cannot quietly compete, and the review process improves the real conversation rather than only the document library.

Treat a temporary article as a controlled bridge. If the source owner needs time to confirm a change, mark the interim instruction with a narrow scope, approving owner, and expiry of its own. State what it does not answer and route unanswered questions. At review, compare the bridge with calls that used it: did agents find the stop rule, did customers receive consistent explanations, and did the linked source support the wording? Retire the bridge when approved guidance is ready so expiry remains a practical call center control rather than a second permanent library.

Use queue changes as a review signal even when no policy changed. A new call reason, transfer destination, field, or approval owner can make an article misleading. Ask the queue owner to walk through one ordinary case and one exception after every material change. If the owner cannot tell which instruction governs, mark the conflict and route it before production use. This connects article expiry to the real operation and gives agents permission to surface uncertainty early.

Keep a simple replacement record: old article, reason for expiry, affected queues, interim route, approving owner, and effective replacement. That record helps a supervisor answer why an agent used a temporary instruction and lets quality review compare calls across the change. It should remain an internal control record, while the public article contains only the guidance customers and representatives are meant to use. Clear separation prevents maintenance details from leaking into customer-facing copy.

Give the frontline team credit for finding stale guidance. A flag that reaches an owner quickly is a useful control, not a failure to memorize the library. Share the replacement or clarification back with the queue and retire the conflicting example. When agents see that uncertainty produces a response, they are more likely to stop safely and less likely to hide a mismatch behind a confident answer.
